# Performance determinants across different biathlon competitions
Laaksonen (Mid Sweden Univ.). Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 5, Feb 2026, pp. 14-17. DOI 10.71006/trnn7439. Read in full (4 pages). A review of how skiing speed, shooting accuracy and range time decide each race format.

- Sprint: skiing speed dominates for lower-ranked athletes (over a big field); among the top, shooting accuracy matters more. Podium finishers typically miss 0-1 targets, 4th-10th about 1-2. Short range time in standing helps.
- Individual: shooting accuracy matters more than in sprint, because each miss costs a minute; the number of misses is the key variable among the best. Efficient pacing matters.
- Pursuit: shooting accuracy explains around 55-60% of variance in race times; start position (sprint result) plus shooting can explain up to 80% of the result. The last (fourth) standing stage has the strongest link to podium rank; one extra miss moves an athlete out of the podium group in 40% to 73% of cases. Podium finishers are slightly faster skiers, but the practical effect is small.
- Mass start: shooting accuracy clearly separates groups (podium: 1 miss; 4th-10th: 2; 11th-20th: 3). Again the last standing stage is the strongest link. Pace matters least in the first lap (simultaneous start).
- Relay: team composition and leg order. Women: reliable shooting and fast range times, especially legs 2 and 4 (spare-round use); men: skiing speed in legs 3 and 4.
- Pacing: a J-shaped curve (fastest early) in individual starts; a more even pace may help athletes who start too fast.

Recommendations for coaches (the author's): Sprint: skiing speed, even pacing, standing shooting and standing range time. Individual: overall shooting accuracy and efficient pacing. Pursuit: the sprint result sets the start position; focus on the last standing stage. Mass start: shooting accuracy and a controlled pace before the key shooting bouts. Relay: as above.

Season relevance: tells the coach which shooting matters most in which race, so race-specific shooting work (last standing stage, range time) in the competition phase.

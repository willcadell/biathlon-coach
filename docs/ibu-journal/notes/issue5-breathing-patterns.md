# Breathing patterns in biathlon shooting: stability as the key to accuracy
Krüger, Harbour & Finkenzeller (Univ. Salzburg). Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 5, Feb 2026, pp. 26-29. DOI 10.71006/pmcs3564. Read in full (4 pages).

Study: 20 athletes (11 elite, IBU Cup / World Cup; 9 junior, national level). Roller-ski complex with four load stages, from aerobic endurance to near-maximal; five prone shots after each load, then a short stadium loop, then five standing. Breathing measured with smart shirts. Pattern = where in the breath cycle the shot broke: inhaled (75-100% of peak volume), neutral (25-75%), exhaled (0-25%). Stability judged by four things: consistency of cycles between shots, of depth at release, of the dominant pattern, and of the apnoea (flat pause) before firing.

Findings:
- Older teaching says fire after about two-thirds of the breath is out. Practice has moved on; the paper asks whether it still matters.
- Prone: elites mostly shot on the exhale (73%), juniors mostly neutral (55%). Standing: both mostly neutral. Shots on the inhale were rare (about 10%).
- Prone: the steadier the breathing pattern across shots, the higher the hit rate (strong, p < .001). Neutral showed a slight positive trend. Higher intensity cut accuracy slightly, not significantly.
- Standing: elites clearly more accurate than juniors (83% vs 69%). Shooting on the inhale hurt: about 13% lower accuracy overall and 23% among juniors. Stability varied with intensity but did not predict accuracy directly.
- Physiology table: inhaling raises arousal and tension; exhaling calms motor control; the neutral pause sits between.

Recommendations for coaches: treat breathing as a structured skill within technical training; train and monitor breathing patterns with tools or observation; start junior athletes early on breathing control.

Evidence: small observational study, roller-ski not snow, hit rate from five-shot sets. Good but modest; the stability-accuracy link prone is the strongest result.

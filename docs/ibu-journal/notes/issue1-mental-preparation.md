# Ready, Set, Go! Mental preparation for competitions
Heinrich (sport psychologist, Univ. of Konstanz). Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 1, Jan 2024, pp. 18-19. DOI 10.71006/ksrw3471. Read in full (2 pages). Two practical techniques; applied-practice article, no study.

1. Mental pre-start routine: alongside the visible routines (warm-up, shooting), a written plan for attention and arousal, made with a sport psychologist. Questions: where should my focus be (wide or narrow) at each point before the start; when should I raise or lower arousal; which tools (self-talk, breathing techniques, music, imagery). Example sequence: shooting at the zero with high, narrow focus; testing skis, relaxing and chatting; warm-up with relaxing music; positive self-talk when collecting the transponder; second warm-up with activating music; imagery of the shooting with relaxed breathing when taking off the jacket; high but wide focus on the start line. Practise it in test competitions, then review and adapt.
2. Motor imagery: rehearse the action in the mind without moving, including the conditions (venue, audience). PETTLEP model: physical position, environment, task, timing (real-time duration), learning (update as skill changes), emotion, perspective (first person). Example: imagine tomorrow's first shooting bout from the last 50 m on skis, onto the mat, the best prone series, leaving the mat; use all senses. Both techniques help regulate attention and get familiar with situations before and during competition, and work only if practised regularly.

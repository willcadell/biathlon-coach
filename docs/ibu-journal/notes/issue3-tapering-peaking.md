# Tapering and peaking for biathlon competition
Mujika (Univ. of the Basque Country). Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 3, Jan 2025, pp. 18-22. DOI 10.71006/skip4500. Read pp. 18-22 (5 pages; the last page, on single versus multiple peaks, only skimmed at thumbnail size). Physical training load, not shooting: useful here only for the season's structure.

- Season structure (Figure 4): preparatory phase, then a competitive phase with several tapers and peaks, then transition. A high "fitness platform" is built in preparation; each important competition block gets a short taper.
- Taper: about two weeks (individually one to four); keep intensity (even slightly up); cut volume by about 41-60%; keep frequency within about 20% of normal so technique and movement efficiency stay sharp; reduce progressively rather than in one step. Fatigue clears, adaptations stay, and good quality work can be done while feeling fresher.
- Recovery, nutrition, hydration, sleep matter more in a taper; time-zone and altitude changes need allowance in the plan (reduced load for altitude adaptation is not itself a taper).
- Multiple peaks across a long competitive period are possible but each needs its own taper and a high platform underneath.

For the shooting library: gives the phase vocabulary (preparatory, competitive, transition; taper before key races). Nothing shooting-specific was read.

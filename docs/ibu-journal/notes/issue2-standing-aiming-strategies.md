# Optimising biathlon standing shooting: insights into aiming strategies
Miika Köykkä. Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 2, Jul 2024, pp. 26-29. DOI 10.71006/dhfa7074. Read in full (4 pages).

Framework: standing shooters approach the target in one of two ways, seen in the aim-point path in the last fraction of a second before the trigger.
- HOLD: settle the aim inside the hit area and keep it still briefly, then trigger. What matters: hold stability inside the hit area and aiming accuracy at the centre.
- TIMING: approach the target straight and pull the trigger while the aim is still moving in towards the centre, without fully settling. What matters: timing the pull to the best aiming picture, with a sensible approach speed that slows right before the shot.
Both work; the study found no performance difference between them. Whatever the strategy, the aim must be under control before the trigger. Four key technical factors in standing: hold stability, aiming accuracy, cleanness of triggering (no extra movement in the final phase), timing of triggering. Sway makes all of these harder. Best judged over several 5-shot sets, not single shots, with aim-trace devices (Noptel, Scatt).

Coaching implications:
- Have a clear technical routine as the foundation; the aiming approach is part of it.
- Holders: improve the ability to settle the hold and trigger just after it stabilises. Static-hold training, with variations, and practise stopping the aim-point movement. Holding longer and longer tends to get worse, not better, especially holding breath under hard effort; if the picture is poor, come off and re-approach after a fresh breath.
- Timers: practise a straightforward approach, slowing down toward the target and triggering when the aim is inside but still moving to the centre. Exercises: follow a line with the aim, practise speeding up and slowing down the aim point for task-specific control, practise timing with a support under the rifle. Timing is not a way to compensate for a poor hold ("throwing" the aim in and pulling quickly does not work). Bring the aim near the target while still breathing, then approach while holding the breath. Less experienced standing shooters often start far from the target, especially on the first shot, which leads to long breath holds and uncontrolled approaches: avoid.
- Succeeding at something unpractised is unlikely: switching to a hold after a failed timing approach rarely works unless practised. Light and high intensity sessions should use the same strategy, since reps with the other strategy will always be far fewer.
- In practice it is a continuum, and skilled athletes adapt it to how they feel (exhausted, breathing hard, nervous) and to wind, light, cold and crowd. Beginners are best to stay with whatever feels most natural, and learn to adapt as skill grows.

Evidence: one framework study (Köykkä et al. 2021, 5-shot sets, standing) plus follow-up work and the author's coaching experience.

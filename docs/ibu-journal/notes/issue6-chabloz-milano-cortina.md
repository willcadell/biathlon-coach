# From preparation to performance: Jean-Marc Chabloz's learnings from Milano Cortina 2026
Interview by Sattlecker. Journal of Biathlon Coaching, Issue 6, Jun 2026, pp. 6-9. DOI 10.71006/HHGV1360. Read in full (4 pages). Chabloz: former shooting coach, Swedish team (to 2026), IBU mentor. Practitioner opinion.

- Adaptability separated the best teams: altitude and low air pressure made shooting conditions variable, mostly hurting first-shot preparation and shooting rhythm. Teams that trusted their preparation and focused on what they could control did best, adjusting race plans when training could not go as planned.
- Olympic season differs from a World Cup season: a four-year cycle aimed at one peak; altitude plays a role in adapting skiing and shooting.
- The approach into the range was decisive and individual: some athletes shoot best entering fast, others need a controlled entry. In head-to-head situations, athletes who held their own pace did better than those reacting to rivals.
- Shooting efficiency (range time) has become decisive: top athletes combine high accuracy with short range times; coaches should develop efficiency, stability and accuracy from a young age.
- Acceptance as a trainable skill: be ready for missed shots, bad weather, early mistakes; athletes who accept the situation (not give up) recover faster; in shooting, let go of a miss and fully re-engage for the next bout.
- Long-term planning, trust in the team, staying curious.

Recommendations for coaches (the interview's list; paraphrased): train shooting efficiency, not only accuracy, from a young age (build speed into the shot process alongside stability, so it holds under race stress); individualise the approach into the range; teach acceptance by rehearsing setbacks deliberately (a miss, a wind shift, an early mistake) so athletes can refocus and re-engage for the next bout; plan in four-year cycles, working back from the target date (altitude, competitions, travel, recovery); protect the plan when conditions break it and focus on controllables; manage logistics, scheduling, equipment and recovery as performance factors; build team culture before it is needed; keep learning.
